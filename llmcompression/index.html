LLM-Based Lossless Text Compression

Shannon's source coding theorem says a good predictor is a good compressor. Modern language models are trained on exactly the objective that makes them good predictors, so pairing one with arithmetic coding should compress well. It does. The interesting question is what it costs, and that is the part the earlier work skipped.

Read the write-up GitHub

What we measured

01

Five models, GPT-2, Llama-3.2-1B, Llama-3.2-3B, Qwen3-0.6B and Qwen3-1.7B, run over a UTF-8 text subset of the Canterbury Corpus. Each model's next-token distribution drives an arithmetic coder, so the compression ratio falls straight out of the model's cross-entropy on the file.

The ratios are large. Average compression lands between 5× and 16× depending on the model, reaching 39× on individual files, against 2× to 4× for gzip and zstd on the same data.

Throughput is where it falls apart. The same pipeline runs 100× to 1000× slower than the classical compressors it beats. That number is the finding, not a footnote: it is the reason this technique is not sitting inside your filesystem, and any writeup that reports only the ratio is telling you half of the result.

Ratio against throughput

Both axes are logarithmic, and the gap on the horizontal one is the whole story. Moving right to left buys you roughly a factor of four in compression and costs you two to three orders of magnitude in speed. Whether that trade is worth making depends entirely on whether you are storing something once and reading it rarely.

Making it fast enough to measure

02

A naive implementation is slow enough that the throughput question cannot be answered honestly, because you end up measuring your own overhead instead of the method. Three things carried most of the difference.

Static KV caching keeps the attention cache in a fixed allocation so the shape stops changing between tokens. CUDA graph capture records the per-token forward pass once and replays it, which removes the kernel launch overhead that otherwise dominates when each step is small. Batched segment processing splits the file and runs segments together, trading a little compression ratio at the seams for parallelism.
