About

01

I work on quantum algorithms and machine learning. Most of my time goes into writing simulators, fitting models to experimental data, and checking whether a speedup that works on paper still holds up on real hardware.

Before I moved into quantum I spent a few years on applied ML and systems work: fine-tuning language models for policy questions, building distributed data pipelines, and running security telemetry at a fintech. I kept that range on purpose, since the problems I find interesting usually need theory and engineering at the same time.

Auto-tuning a double quantum dot

a toy of what my thesis automates

A constant-interaction model of a silicon double quantum dot, read out through a simulated charge sensor. The tuner never sees the charge numbers, only the sensor. It empties both dots, adds one electron to each by counting charge transitions with a change-point detector, then centres the (1,1) operating point and holds it. Drag on the diagram to add charge noise and watch it re-tune. Parameters are illustrative; the thesis works on real devices at Diraq and UNSW.
